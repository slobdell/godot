# Voice clips (the recorded announcer)

777 Ogg Vorbis clips, 30 minutes of speech, 15 MB. Generated by `make announcer-generate APPROVED=1` from
`../lines.json`; the masters they were cut from are git-ignored (220 MB) and can be re-cut without spending credits.

**Godot deliberately ignores this folder** (`.gdignore`). Nothing here is a Godot resource:

- `AnnouncerVoice` loads clips with `AudioStreamOggVorbis.load_from_file()` at runtime, by path, so they never
  need importing — and importing 777 files crashed the editor's import step on the laptop.
- They are excluded from both export presets. At 15 MB they would be nineteen times the rest of the web pack, and
  the design was always that a core pack ships and bigger voice packs arrive afterwards (game_design.md
  *The pipeline*, step 5). `--announcer-clips=DIR` points the booth at wherever the pack actually lives.

`manifest.json` **is** read as a plain file too, and is the only thing the game needs to know what is here.

## What is in a clip id

| Form | Example | What |
|---|---|---|
| `<line id>` | `pa.welcome.03` | a whole line with no slots, said in one piece |
| `<line id>#<n>` | `caller.kill.52#0` | a carrier segment of a line that has slots |
| `fill.<speaker>.<vocab>.<value>.<intonation>` | `fill.caller.team.rust.mid` | one slot value, in the intonation the line needs |

## Verifying them

`make announcer-generate` speech-to-text checks every clip as it is cut, but the recogniser cannot fairly judge a
fragment or a lone short word out of context — it heard "is down to" as "This down" and "Rust" as nothing, then
transcribed both perfectly once stitched. Those are reported as unverifiable and checked by
**`make announcer-stitch-check`**, which assembles each filler into a real line and transcribes the assembly. That
is also the only check that can catch a bad *slice*, which sounds fine alone and wrong in a sentence.
